🛡️ INVESTIGATIVE RESPONSIBILITY

MPCTC 04.01.00
  • Patrol / Precinct Responsibility: Unless otherwise directed, precincts (patrol officers) are responsible for the preliminary investigation of all reported crimes and incidents in Baltimore County.
  • Duty to Investigate: When an officer is made aware of an incident that occurred in Baltimore County, it is the officer's responsibility to investigate the incident and, if necessary, file a report.
  • Primary Responsibility: Perform a thorough investigation in an attempt to develop information leading to the arrest of the perpetrator.
  • Secondary Responsibility: Instruct the victim in crime prevention techniques to reduce the chance of a future recurrence of the incident.
  • When Preliminary Investigation Begins & Ends:
    • Begins: When officers arrive at the scene of an incident.
    • Continues: Until such time as postponement of the investigation or the transfer of responsibility will not jeopardize the successful completion of the investigation.

🚨 PRELIMINARY INVESTIGATION STEPS & SCENE SECURITY

MPCTC 04.02.00 – 04.02.04 • 04.06.02 • 04.12.00 • 04.20.03
  1. Provide Aid to the Injured: Scene security first! Use Personal Protective Equipment (PPE), eliminate the threat, relocate victims, triage, and request medics.
  2. Protect the Incident Scene to Prevent Loss/Contamination of Evidence (04.02.01, 04.02.04):
    • Crime Scene Perimeter (04.02.04): The general area in and around the largest body of evidence.
    • Block entry and exit points and use crime scene tape.
    • Delegate an officer to log everyone entering the scene.
    • Limit access to essential personnel only.
    • Designate entry and exit paths for personnel so it will not contaminate the scene; account for weather considerations.
  3. Determine if an Incident Occurred & Its Exact Nature/Circumstances: Did it occur in Baltimore County? Criminal vs. Civil? Felony vs. Misdemeanor? Robbery vs. Burglary? Rape vs. Sex Offense? Who has investigative responsibility?
  4. Determine Identity of Suspects & Effect Arrest at Scene or via Immediate Pursuit (04.20.03): Establish a perimeter, broadcast a description, review video surveillance, request additional resources (Aviation, K-9, etc.), interview victim/witnesses.
    5 Methods for Tracking a Suspect from a Crime Scene (04.20.03): K-9, Blood Trails, Footprints, Tire Marks, Witnesses.
  5. Furnish Other Field Units Relevant Info on Wanted Persons/Vehicles: Via Radio, MDT, Department Cell Phone, License Plate Reader (LPR), eBooking, MVA.
  6. Obtain Complete Identification of All Victims & Witnesses (04.02.03): Allows positive ID to locate/interview witnesses later, allows witnesses to be summonsed to court, and witnesses cannot leave if you are holding their ID.
  7. Determine & Evaluate Information Known by Victims/Witnesses (04.02.02, 04.06.02):
    • Separate witnesses as soon as practical (04.02.02).
    • Document all information no matter how insignificant it seems; use your BWC.
    • Selective Neighborhood Check / Canvass (04.06.02): Start with buildings/residences that have a view of the crime scene (knock on doors). Some witnesses may have seen the suspect or crime without knowing it. Document who you interviewed AND what addresses were attempted with no answer so follow-up can be attempted.
  8. Arrange for the Collection of Evidence & Obtain Written Statements from victims and witnesses.
  9. Accurately & Completely Record All Pertinent Info on an Incident Report: Official record of what occurred; basis for follow-up; used by prosecutor and defense in court testimony.
  10. Provide Assistance/Referral Services & Crime Prevention: Domestic Violence Coordinator, State's Attorney, issue Victim/Witness brochure, Security Survey, empower victim to reduce fear of crime.
  11. Answer the 5 W's & H (Who, What, When, Where, Why, How): Motive (04.12.00) is defined as "something that causes a person to act."

🔍 FOLLOW-UP INVESTIGATIONS & SPECIALIZED UNIT MATRIX

  • Default Follow-Up Responsibility: Handled at the precinct level (by the investigating officer or precinct Investigative Services Team [IST] detectives) until cleared or no longer feasible, except as noted in the Administrative Manual.
  • Notification Prior to Charging: When an arrest is made, prior to charging the suspect, the investigating officer must notify the supervisor of the unit having follow-up responsibility for the crime under investigation.
  • Bribery or Felony Extortion: The CIB Commander will be notified to determine which CIB unit will have follow-up responsibility on a case-by-case basis.
  • Suspended Cases / Additional Info: Cases temporarily suspended after preliminary investigation will be reassigned for follow-up if additional info is received. Members receiving additional info must file an Incident Supplement, and their supervisor ensures active follow-up.
  • Outside Boundaries: Cases requiring extensive investigation outside precinct or jurisdictional boundaries may remain a precinct-level investigation at the discretion of the member's supervisor or commander.

📋 MANDATORY INCIDENT REPORTS & CASE CLEARANCE

6 Calls Requiring an Incident Report

  1. Calls of a criminal nature.
  2. Violent, criminal, or disorderly calls at premises holding a Baltimore County liquor license (for dispensing/consuming on premises).
  3. Violent, criminal, or disorderly calls involving a licensed medical marijuana grower, processor, or dispensary.
  4. Vulnerable adult/elder abuse, sexual assault, child abuse, or domestic abuse — whether founded or unfounded.
  5. Animal abuse, animal cruelty, and animal neglect — whether founded or unfounded.
  6. Calls occurring on the premises of a hookah lounge/bar, or resulting from the operation of the business.

5 Ways a Case is "Cleared"

  1. Suspect has been identified and there will be no prosecution by the victim.
  2. Suspect has been identified and the victim is instructed to obtain a warrant or summons.
  3. Suspect has been arrested.
  4. Suspect has been granted immunity.
  5. Abated by death (suspect is deceased).

🧠 MEMORY STAGES & WHY NOTES MATTER

3 Stages of Memory

Human memory is complex with incredible capacity, but fragile and susceptible to error. You cannot depend on it alone!

  1. Acquisition: Transforming info into a form a person can retain (requires deliberately forming a mental image at the time of occurrence).
  2. Retention: Storing information over time.
  3. Retrieval: Recalling stored information.

What Affects Memory: Individual abilities, training, emphasis placed on event, level of interest, interference (other things going on), time of day, adequate sleep & nutrition.

Notes in Court & Testimony (07.04.02)

  • "If it's not on paper, it didn't happen."
  • Notes may become evidence in court under "Discovery".
  • Type reports from both notes AND BWC so nothing is left out.
  • Officers can refer to notes while testifying, eliminating uncertainty ("I don't recall", "I don't remember", "I'm not sure").
  • Counteracts Denials: Defendant cannot change their statement; corroborates officer's actions & witness statements; counters hostile witnesses.
  • Traffic Court: Citations blend together; if a driver fails to appear (FTA), court may be months or years later.

⭐ ★ FIELD NOTE POLICY, MINIMUM CONTENTS & INTERVIEW TECHNIQUES

★ HIGH-PRIORITY EXAM ITEMS

★ Baltimore County Field Manual Article 1, Section 3.1 (MPCTC 07.04.01 & 07.04.05)

  • When Investigative Notes Must Be Maintained (07.04.05): Investigative notes consist of notes taken during investigations which will be transcribed onto the official report. They only need to be maintained for criminal cases and motor vehicle crashes when an ACRS report has been completed.
  • Required Header on Every Page (07.04.01): Each page must contain your Name, ID#, and Case Number (CC#).
  • Retention: Each officer is responsible for safely and permanently retaining these notes.
  • If No Notes Are Taken: The officer must document why in his/her police report.

★ What to Write Down in Notes (07.04.01)

  • Significant conditions at time of arrival
  • Date/time of occurrence & CC number
  • Name, sex, DOB, address, & phone number of all people involved (Victims, Suspects, Witnesses; in Domestic cases, children's names must be listed)
  • Chronological account of actions & Sketch
  • Circumstances & Motive, visible injuries vs. complaint of pain
  • Actual statements made (language utilized / responses received)
  • Identification & handling of evidence (who found what, where, and when; who took photos)
  • Internal & external resources that responded (Crime Lab, Supervisor, Detective)

★ Note-Taking Techniques That Won't Discourage Talking (07.04.04)

  1. Tell the person that you will be taking notes.
  2. Look up from your notebook.
  3. Be patient and allow the person to completely and thoughtfully answer each question without interruption.
  4. Reassure the person that they are providing useful information.

Tactics to Verify Field Interview Info:

1. Separate multiple witnesses, victims, and suspects • 2. Compare information • 3. Verify information with other sources.

📈 ★ TRUTH VERIFICATION DEVICES (POLYGRAPH & CVSA)

★ MPCTC 04.08.00 – 04.08.02 • Field Manual 7-11.0
  • Most Common Forms: Polygraphs and Computer Voice Stress Analysis (CVSA).
  • How They Work: Truth verification systems do NOT actually detect lies; they measure the stress caused by those lies.
  • ★ Role in an Investigation (04.08.00): Provides a psychological advantage based on the test subject's perception of the procedure's ability to determine truth from lies. When used properly by a trained examiner who understands the psychology of convincing a subject that the truth will be revealed, the end result is "A confession."
  • Polygraph Scheduling & Prerequisites (Field Manual 7-11.0): Requested by contacting the examiner personally or by phone for an appointment. Will not be given until sufficient facts of the investigation have been established.
  • 3 Requirements to Polygraph a Crime Victim (Field Manual 7-11.0):
    1. Documented evidence that the alleged offense may be a false report.
    2. Approval of the investigating shift/unit commander.
    3. Prior consultation with the State's Attorney's Office.
  • ★ CRITICAL EXCEPTION (Rape & Sexual Offenses): Victims of first and second degree rape and all degrees of sexual offenses will NEVER be required to submit to a polygraph examination as a condition for proceeding with the investigation of a sexual offense.
  • ★ Possible Pitfalls of Using on Victims (04.08.01): (1) Psychological effects on the truthful victim, and (2) Police Department is perceived as insensitive.
  • ★ Limitations in an Investigation (04.08.02): (1) Time consuming, and (2) Accuracy rates are less than 100%.
Lesson 6

Identification Procedures

MPCTC 04.10.00 – 04.10.01 • Special Order #2017-03

👁️ VISUAL INVESTIGATIVE AIDS — STRICT NEUTRALITY RULES

  • Conducted in a structured, professional manner designed to clear the innocent as well as identify suspects.
  • Never Sole Basis: Criminal investigations should NOT be concluded or ceased based solely on eyewitness identification.
  • No Opinions or Suggestive Comments: Investigating officers must NOT, by word or gesture, offer opinions or make suggestive comments to any victim/witness concerning the guilt or innocence of a subject before, during, or after any identification procedure. (Victims/witnesses asking for a member's opinion shall be informed of this restriction.)
  • No Feedback: Officers are prohibited from providing any feedback to the victim/witness regarding the results of the identification.
  • Record Confidence & Comments: Officers must record any/all victim/witness comments and relevant observations, including their level of confidence, when making or not making an identification.
  • Robbery Unit Cases: Always ask Robbery Unit permission first before conducting any show-ups / one-on-ones!

👥 LINE-UPS, SHOW-UPS, PHOTO ARRAYS & COMPOSITES

1. Line-ups (Rarely Used — Coordinated by Robbery Unit)

  • All lineups are coordinated and executed by the Robbery Unit (requested by SAO or investigating officer with supervisor approval).
  • The assigned Robbery supervisor has complete authority over the process and can cancel, postpone, or make changes at any time for safety and integrity.
  • Who Can Be Used as Extras / Fill-ins: Sworn members, Cadets, Recruits, or BCDC Prisoners (prisoners receive $4, a phone call, and refreshments). Must be similar in race, sex, age, physical stature, and attire; props may be used.
  • Key Lineup Rules:
    • Only ONE suspect shall be shown in each lineup.
    • When showing a new suspect to the same witness, new fill-ins must be used.
    • The suspect may choose whatever position they desire.
    • Crime Lab will be present and video record each lineup.
    • Witnesses will be kept separated during the process.

2. Show-Ups ("One-on-One" Identification — MPCTC 04.10.01)

  • Presented within a reasonable length of time and proximity to the crime when a detained subject fits the witness's description.
  • When Prohibited: If a suspect held in custody has been officially charged, a show-up will NOT be conducted.
  • Witness Rules: Never conduct with more than one victim/witness present at a time. Never present the same suspect to the same witness more than once. The witness must advise officers prior to the show-up that they will be able to recognize the perpetrator.
  • Detaining Suspect: Detain at the place located in the least restrictive manner possible that ensures the suspect remains with the officer.
  • Form #126 (Witness Identification Admonition): Complete PRIOR to conducting a show-up (or photo array). Officer reads each admonition to the witness; witness initials after reading, writes any comments, and signs the form.
  • Transport Direction: ALWAYS bring the witness to the suspect — DO NOT bring the suspect to the witness! Shield the witness from being seen by the suspect.
  • If Not Identified: If the detained subject is not identified and officers lack other probable cause, release the subject and complete an Incident Supplement.
  • 6 Required Items on the Show-Up Incident Supplement (Completed by Transporting Officer whether or not charges are filed):
    1. Name of the officer(s) who detained the possible suspect
    2. Date, time, and location of the show-up
    3. Full information of the detained possible suspect (name, gender, race, DOB, address, physical description)
    4. Results of the show-up and any statements made
    5. Name of the victim/witness who viewed the possible suspect
    6. Name of the officer who transported the victim/witness and the type of transportation (marked, unmarked, nondescript, etc.)

3. Single Photo ID (Last Resort)

  • Acceptable only in very limited circumstances: when the victim/witness is familiar with the suspect and you need to verify identity.
  • Victim/witness signs the photo.
  • Photo is packaged as evidence.

4. Forensic Artist Composites

  • Requested by investigating officer with supervisor approval.
  • Contact the Robbery Unit.
  • Only created by approved forensic artists (surrounding jurisdictions may be used).
  • Case Study: 1995 OKC Bombing (Ryder truck axle VIN → composite → Tim McVeigh).

5. Mobile Fingerprint ID (SO #2017-03)

  • Used by trained member with shift commander approval.
  • Used when subject is under arrest, gives consent, or search warrant, or for deceased, unconscious, or incapable of consent.
  • Not a replacement for routine arrest fingerprinting; document in report.
Lesson 10

Found Property & Property Forms/Tags

MPCTC 04.15.00–03 • 04.16.02–03 • 04.17.00 • 06.05.09

🏷️ PROPERTY FORMS, COPIES & THE 30-DAY FINDER CLAIM RULE

Required Forms for All Found Property

  • Incident Report (or Incident Supplement)
  • Form #15 (Property Sheet):
    • Front: Always completed when taking custody.
    • Back (Rear): Completed & signed by owner to release property back to owner.
    • 3 Copies Required: Standard Found Property calls.
    • 4 Copies Required: Firearms and CDS (Narcotics).
  • Form #128 / Form #128D: Disposition of Property/Evidence.
  • Appropriate Tag: Miscellaneous Property Tag or Firearms Evidence Tag.
  • End of Tour Rule: All found property must be properly packaged and stored prior to the end of your tour of duty.

30-Day Finder Claim Rule (MPCTC 06.05.09)

  • At the end of 30 days, if the owner cannot be located, the finder can claim the property (unless prohibited).
  • Officer Exclusion: BCoPD members finding property in Baltimore County, on or off duty, have NO right to claim found property.
  • Documenting Finder's Intent (Done 4 Times):
    1. In the narrative of your Incident Report
    2. On the Form #15 Property Sheet
    3. On the Miscellaneous Property Tag
    4. On Form #128D (indicating finder wishes to claim after 30 days)
  • Property in Dispute: Will NOT be given to any claimant unless all other claimants renounce all claims in writing or upon order by the Chief of Police.

Cannot Be Claimed by Finder (Sold at Auction or Destroyed)

1. Guns • 2. Motorized Vehicles (title issues) • 3. Boats (title issues) • 4. CDS or related contraband • 5. Weapons of any type.

May NEVER Be Claimed by Owner OR Finder (MPCTC 04.15.03)

1. CDS (Controlled Dangerous Substances) and 2. Contraband.

🔫 ALCOHOL, NARCOTICS, FIREARMS & EVICTIONS

Alcoholic Beverages

  • Disposed of at the precinct by emptying contents into an appropriate drain and discarding containers in a trash can.
  • Take a photograph of the container prior to disposal and submit via BWC to Evidence.com (photo becomes your "evidence").
  • Document in Incident Report narrative: (1) "the basis for their belief that the containers do, in fact, contain alcoholic beverages" and (2) the manner of disposal.
  • Notify your supervisor of the actions taken.

Found Narcotics (CDS)

  • Requires an Incident Report.
  • Requires a total of four (4) Form #15 Property Sheets.
  • Must be seized, properly packaged according to Departmental regulations, and submitted to EMU.
  • Complete a Form #128D stating that the property is to be destroyed.

Found Firearms & Release Procedures (MPCTC 04.17.00)

  • Safety & Unloading: Point in a safe direction and completely unload. For automatic/semi-automatic weapons, after removing the magazine, the chamber must also be cleared. If unsure how to unload, contact the Firearms Interdiction Team (FIT) or Forensic Services Section.
  • Witness Signature: After the firearm is clear and safe, have a witness officer sign Form #15.
  • Form #15 & Tagging: Requires 4 copies of Form #15. Multiple firearms and related evidence (magazines, casings, ammo, extra barrels) can be on the same Form #15, but must be on a separate Form #15 from any other category of property/evidence. Wire a Firearms Evidence Tag securely to the trigger guard or grip area to the rear of the trigger.
  • NEVER Release Firearms at Precinct (04.17.00): Officers may NEVER release any firearm back to the owner! All firearms are released exclusively by the Evidence Management Unit (EMU).
  • 8 Firearm Claimant Qualification Requirements: (1) Proof of positive ownership • (2) Not convicted of a crime of violence • (3) Not convicted of a misdemeanor crime of domestic violence (18 U.S.C.) • (4) Not a fugitive from justice • (5) Not an unlawful user of/addicted to alcohol, marijuana, depressants, stimulants, or narcotics • (6) Not adjudicated mentally defective or committed to a mental institution • (7) At least 21 for handguns or 18 for long guns • (8) Firearm is not illegal/contraband.
  • Release / Denial Workflow:
    • Officer completes Incident Supplement (Firearms Evidence: Disposition) listing what background info could/could not be accessed + attaches all database printouts.
    • If Qualified: Include claimant's full name, DOB, address on Supplement + complete Form #128D ("Property Released To") → forwarded to FIT for in-depth background check → IBIS test-fire (all handguns & most long guns test-fired, takes 1–1.5 hrs/gun) → EMU notifies claimant by certified mail to pick up at Public Safety Building.
    • If Denied: Document reasons for denial on Supplement + complete Form #128 ("Property May be Disposed of") → EMU notifies claimant & attorney of right to petition the District Court of Maryland within 30 days.

Special Property Rules

  • Body Fluids / Contamination: Distinctly label "Biohazard".
  • Perishable Property: Promptly refrigerate/care for and report to shift commander.
  • Animals, Pushcarts, Large/Heavy Items: Transport to station/safe place as directed by OIC; if owner not found in reasonable time, refer to commander.
  • Large Cash, CDS, Valuables, Volatile Items: Contact EMU for direction.
  • Trucks with Valuable Cargo in Accident: Contact trucking and insurance companies ASAP.

Evictions — 5 Categories to Take Into Custody

If the owner cannot be located or refuses to take responsibility:

  1. Small loose valuables (cash, negotiable papers, credit cards, jewelry)
  2. Dangerous items (guns, explosives, ammunition)
  3. Contraband, drugs (including prescription meds), and alcohol
  4. Medicines and poisons
  5. Any items that may be hazardous
