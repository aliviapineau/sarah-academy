Part 2

16 Circumstances for a Warrantless Traffic Arrest

When an Officer Can Effect a Warrantless Arrest for a Traffic Violation

Your study guide lists 16 specific circumstances under the Transportation Article (Title 26) where an officer may make a warrantless arrest for a traffic violation:

DUI, Hit & Run, and Serious Crashes (6)

  1. DUI Alcohol
  2. DUI Drugs
  3. Hit & Run (Injury or Death) — failure to render reasonable assistance
  4. Hit & Run (Property Damage) — failure to notify
  5. Crash that caused bodily injury or death
  6. Non-resident commits a violation and it resulted in a crash

License, Identity & Citation Compliance (5)

  1. Driving w/o a license
  2. Driving w/ suspended or revoked license
  3. Does not furnish satisfactory evidence of identity
  4. Refusal to sign a citation
  5. Disregard a traffic citation

Fleeing, Racing & MVA Document Fraud (3)

  1. Fleeing and eluding
  2. Race or speed contest
  3. Falsification of a title, registration card/plate, or any official MVA documents

Hazmat & Weighing Stations (2)

  1. Relating to vehicles transporting hazardous materials
  2. Weighing Station (refusal to weigh) or to remove excess weight from it
Part 3

Traffic Enforcement Principles, Options, Pacing & Complaints

Why Enforce Traffic Laws

★ Exam #11 Homework #1
  • Public Safety (1st Priority)
  • Change behavior / Deter aggressive driving
  • Reduce crashes
  • Educate motorists
  • Deter and reduce criminal activity

Enforcement Objectives

★ Exam #8
  • Be proactive in your enforcement
  • Identify problem drivers
  • Remove improper/unsafe vehicles from the road
  • Reduce traffic related crashes
  • Use problem solving approach to traffic complaints
  • Document all traffic enforcement action taken

2 Categories of Enforcement

★ Exam #1
1. Proactive
  • Self-initiated enforcement
2. Reactive (Traffic Complaints)
  • Citizen initiated
  • Statistically generated
  • Government Agency initiated

Guideline for Enforcement

★ Exam #14
  1. Identify that a violation has been committed
  2. Charge violator with most appropriate violation
  3. Take appropriate actions
  4. Enforce fairly/impartially

Discretionary Elements & Professionalism

★ Exam #2 & #15
  • Charges must be based on violations either observed or investigated
  • Charges should be based on seriousness of violation(s), observations by officer & evidence
  • Enforcement action must be appropriate
    • Was your violation tolerance consistent?

Professionalism

  • Be consistent when enforcing traffic laws and during interactions with motorists
  • Refrain from arguing with violators

6 Enforcement Options (Least → Most Punitive)

★ Exam #7
  1. Verbal or written warnings (Least Punitive)
  2. Request for Re-examination (MVA)
  3. Issuance of Parking Citations
  4. Issuance of Safety Equipment Repair Orders (SEROs)
  5. Issuance of Traffic Citations
  6. Physical Arrest (Most Punitive)

How to Pace a Vehicle (5 Steps)

★ Exam #6
  1. Observe a vehicle that appears to be exceeding the speed limit.
  2. Position your vehicle at a safe distance behind the violator.
  3. Follow for at least 3/10 mile, maintain that exact distance between you and the violator, and maintain a constant speed.
  4. Match your speed with the violator's (if speed varies, you must start your pace over).
  5. After at least 3/10 of a mile, you can effect a traffic stop and issue a citation for the speed you paced.

Citizen Traffic Complaint Citations

★ Exam #17

Can you write a citation after another citizen makes a traffic complaint to you? What are the 5 considerations?

  1. Did a violation actually occur?
  2. Can complainant describe/ID vehicle and driver?
  3. Does the complainant desire prosecution?
  4. Is the complainant willing to testify in court?
  5. Based on your interaction with the violator, do you feel a citation is the appropriate enforcement?
Part 4

Selective Enforcement, Bias-Based Profiling & Form #9

Define Selective Enforcement & Its 3 Elements

★ Exam #3

Selective Traffic Law Enforcement is enforcement proportional to traffic accidents with respect to time, place, and type of violation:

3 Elements of Selective Traffic Law Enforcement

  1. Designed to reduce accidents (crashes)
  2. Time and place accidents frequently occur
  3. Types of violations that contribute to those accidents

3 Types of Selective Enforcement

★ Exam #5
  1. 1. Self-initiated
  2. 2. Complaint initiated
  3. 3. Statistically generated

4 Stages of the Selective Enforcement Cycle

★ Exam #4
  1. Visibility — saturation, omnipresence
  2. Deterrence — creating awareness
  3. Compliance — reinforced by enforcement action
  4. Non-Occurrence — never 100%, out of sight out of mind

4-Step Selective Traffic Enforcement Problem-Solving Approach

  1. Identify / Define the problem
  2. Develop an enforcement strategy
  3. Implement enforcement strategy
  4. Evaluate / Follow up

5 Enforcement Strategies

★ Exam #10
  1. Location to stop — safe area to observe violations and safe locations to conduct the stops
  2. Permission needed for private property
  3. Random times & locations vs. Specific
  4. Remain consistent in enforcement actions and interactions with motorists
  5. Make it a positive interaction for the violator

Bias-Based Profiling & Form #9

Bias-Based Profiling: The selection of an individual for any police-initiated action based solely upon assumptions regarding a person's:

1. Race
2. Ethnicity
3. Age
4. Economic Status
5. Religion
6. Cultural Group
